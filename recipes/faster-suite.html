Our suite takes too long — where is the time actually going? ​

The instinct is to look for slow tests. That is usually the smallest part of the bill. A suite's wall clock is mostly made of four things, and only one of them is "the app is slow":

  1. Waits somebody wrote — waitForTimeout sleeps that were a workaround once.
  2. Timeouts nobody revisited — a thirty-second budget on a three-second test costs twenty-seven seconds every time it fails.
  3. Retries of flaky tests — paid on every run, and they buy no signal.
  4. The application itself — the only part that needs a real fix.

Piwi measures all four separately, so you can spend effort where the minutes are. It does not make your app faster; it tells you whether that is even the problem.

1. Split the bill before optimizing anything ​

Analytics has two widgets that belong side by side: CI time, the minutes your runs consumed, and Wasted CI time, the part that produced no signal (what counts as wasted).

If wasted time is a small slice, your suite is just big: look at the application and parallelism. If it is a large slice, start with the waits, the timeouts and the flaky retries.

2. Reclaim the waits you wrote ​

The sleeps somebody wrote are wasted time Piwi totals per execution and per run, and which waits count is yours to tune (wasted time). Turn on Show waits on a run's Timeline tab and each sleep is a bar that opens its test.

Count every wait once to see how much of the suite is waiting on something, then set the patterns back.

3. Tighten the timeouts that only bill you on failure ​

An oversized per-test timeout bills you only when the test fails. The Performance tab's Timeout opportunities lists the tests whose timeout dwarfs their real duration, and the outgrown test.slow() marks, each with a tighter value and the time it reclaims.

Read the percentile line, not the average.

4. Stop paying for flaky retries ​

A flaky test bills you twice, for the failed attempt and for the retry, on every run where it misbehaves. That is why Piwi ranks flaky tests by what their retries cost (impact) rather than by flakiness score.

Cutting the flakiness that costs the most is the whole recipe for this. The short version: work down the impact order, fix the red dots, and quarantine the rest so they stop blocking merges while still running.

5. Ask whether the app is slow, not the test ​

Everything above shaves time off the harness; this step finds a real performance bug.

  • Slow endpoints: on a run, network requests grouped by normalized route with their duration and error rate; Analytics lifts the same view across every project.
  • Web Vitals: TTFB, FCP, LCP, CLS and the rest per execution. LCP, CLS and INP are Chromium-only.

Both require the capture fixtures: the reporter alone cannot see the network. Without them you still have the trace: its network waterfall shows the same requests for one execution, just not aggregated across the suite.

6. Spend the parallelism you already have ​

  • Worker imbalance is called out in a run's Timeline tab, under the worker distribution: if one worker finishes long after the others, the suite is as slow as its unluckiest shard, and no amount of per-test tuning fixes that.
  • Sharding is Playwright's own --shard; Piwi merges the shards back into one run, so you can raise the shard count without turning your history into fragments. See CI & sharding.

Other ways in ​

Ask your agent. Over the MCP server, get_slow_tests returns the ranked slowest tests, get_performance_trend the duration history, and get_run_insights the worker imbalance of one run.

Script it. The same numbers are on the REST API: see the API docs.

Watch it drift. Rather than auditing periodically, subscribe to the perf.regressionnotification event and let it tell you when a duration trend breaks.
